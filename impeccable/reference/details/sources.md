# Sources and overrides

This file gives the source behind each UI rule and number. It also lists every place we chose research or upstream impeccable over the owner's UI/UX video notes ("video" below). Load it when a rule seems to contradict another source, when you need the source behind a number, or when you upgrade impeccable. The rules themselves live in the detail files named in each section.

Two tags mark claims that research did not back:

- Starting value. A number to test and tune. No outside source backs it.
- No source found. Research looked for support and found none, or research did not check the claim. Video-only rules carry this tag.

## Color and surfaces

- Light or dark comes from the use scene. Video said start from a moody photo and darken its crop. Upstream craft-floor.md picks light or dark from the scene. We keep cropping and darkening as a technique, not a default.
- No grain or noise layer. Video said layer noise over the anchor photo. Upstream craft-floor.md bans grain. No source found for grain, so the ban stands.
- Grid textures and frame lines. Video said graph paper, and 1px grids around sections, bento cells, and the hero. Upstream craft-floor.md bans two-axis grids unless a real canvas, map, blueprint, or measuring tool sits under them. We allow 1px lines only on real column or section edges, where they divide content (surfaces.md).
- Grouping by proximity. Video said wrap related items in a shared container. Upstream layout.md says use proximity first. We add a container only where proximity fails.
- Corner radius. Video said heavy radii for warm worlds, sharp radii for modern, and 10px for small components. Upstream craft-floor.md keeps card radii at 12-16px and reserves pills for small controls. We keep 12-16px for cards, let the committed world set the flavor, and keep small controls below the card radius. The 10px start is a starting value.
- Card edges and shadows. Video said light cards use fills instead of outlines, with any border at 15% black. Upstream surfaces.md used a 6% black ring under a lift shadow, and craft-floor.md bans a border under a wide soft shadow. We declare one elevation per card: a border for a flat card, one shadow token for a raised card. [Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/elevation) pairs a border with flat cards. [Refactoring UI, as summarized by updivision](https://updivision.com/blog/post/book-review-refactoring-ui-by-adam-wathan-steve-schoger), prefers shadow, so the card's role decides. Softer, larger shadows mean higher elevation ([Material](https://m1.material.io/material-design/elevation-shadows.html)). The 4-10% shadow alpha and the 15% border are starting values.
- Shadow color. Video said a very light gray shadow. Upstream surfaces.md uses transparent black, and we keep it. Use transparent black at low alpha or a tint of the surface hue, never opaque gray.
- Hard offset shadow. Video had a 100% opacity offset shadow in a darker shade. Upstream craft-floor.md bans hard offset shadows outside a neobrutalist world. We allow one only when the committed world is neobrutalist.
- Glow. Video had a blurred duplicate rectangle behind a card as a glow. Upstream craft-floor.md bans a zero-offset colored halo. We use a heavy accent stroke for focus or selection (surfaces.md).
- Dark surfaces. The canvas is near-black gray, never #000, and higher surfaces are lighter ([Material dark theme](https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme.html) gives 0-16% overlay steps for elevation). The 4-6% gap between dark surfaces is a starting value. Desaturating bright brand colors has no source found, so test contrast first.
- Light canvas. [Material 3](https://m3.material.io/blog/tone-based-surface-color-m3) sets the default canvas at tone 98 with chroma 6, a tinted neutral. Pure white for elevated cards is a starting value. No source found for white cards on an off-white canvas.
- Brand tint. Video said replace pure white and black with extreme brand tints. Upstream colorize.md tints neutrals only when the brand hue adds cohesion, and neutral gray is valid. We keep that condition.
- Semantic hues. Video said blue means trust, with fixed hues. [Carbon](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/core/patterns/status-indicators) uses red for danger, yellow or orange for warning, green for success, and blue for passive notices. The one trust source is a weak blog, so we do not encode trust in blue. Pair every status color with an icon or text.
- 60-30-10. Video said 60-30-10 as the color split. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/color-enhance-design) recommends it with neutral dominant colors. [Jasmine Alley](https://www.jasminealley.com/60-30-10-color-rule) argues it is a hierarchy, not a pie chart. We use it as a hierarchy guide. Product UI runs more neutral than 60%. The 90/8/2 split is a starting value.
- Alpha text and borders. Video used 40% alpha for text and borders. Alpha contrast shifts with the background, and 40% black on white is 2.85:1, under the 3:1 floor for input borders. We use solid tokens: 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for input borders ([WCAG non-text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast)).
- Ramps. Video built steps by changing HSB brightness and saturation. We build ramps in OKLCH, changing lightness and keeping chroma and hue. [W3C CSS Color 4](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-color-4/) calls Oklab perceptually uniform. Existing HSL palettes do not convert automatically. [Tailwind v4](https://tailwindcss.com/blog/tailwindcss-v4) moved its palette to OKLCH.
- Hover and pressed. Each uses a named state token, one ramp step from the base, as a starting value. Whether hover goes lighter or darker has no source found. Disabled controls skip the contrast rule but must stay readable without color ([WCAG non-text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast)).
- Destructive color. Destructive actions use the red danger role with a verb label, never the brand primary color. Sources: [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons), [Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/button/usage), [VA](https://design.va.gov/components/button/), and [Red Hat](https://ux.redhat.com/elements/button/guidelines).
- Nested radius. Inner radius equals outer radius minus padding ([Open Props on CodePen](https://codepen.io/argyleink/pen/LYmpqMB), [30 seconds of code](https://github.com/Chalarangelo/30-seconds-of-code/blob/master/content/snippets/css/s/nested-border-radius.md)). One blog uses half the outer thickness. It is an outlier, so we follow the majority.
- Icon size. Match the line height of the text beside the icon, as a starting value. [Material](https://m3.material.io/styles/icons/designing-icons) sets one stroke weight and square terminals. Sources split on the anchor: [Humbl](https://humbldesign.io/blog-posts/how-to-design-perfect-button) uses cap height, and [damato](https://blog.damato.design/posts/button-size-styles) uses line height. We use line height.
- Button and chip padding. Label-only buttons use horizontal padding of about 1.5 to 2 times the vertical, as a starting value. [Humbl](https://humbldesign.io/blog-posts/how-to-design-perfect-button) gives about 1.6 times. No source found for 2 times or for chip padding.

## Typography

- Serif with sans. Video said pair an expressive serif with a clean sans. Upstream new-work.md lists serif faces as defaults and asks for a reason. We allow the pair when the reason is named. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/pairing-typefaces) says one family can look professional on its own.
- One family first. Video said one clean sans for the whole design. Upstream typeset.md allows a second family with a role of its own, and we keep that. A third family needs a fixed job. The cap of three families has no source found.
- Display and script faces. Use them for headlines and decorative labels, never body text. [Material](https://m3.material.io/styles/typography/applying-type) suggests expressive faces for display type. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/legibility-readability-comprehension) says unusual shapes read worse.
- Type scale. Product UI uses a ratio of 1.125 to 1.2 with 3 or 4 working sizes (upstream operate.md). Marketing pages use 1.25 to 1.333. [Art of Style Frame](https://artofstyleframe.com/blog/type-scale-systems-modular-scale/) calls 1.25 the general default and 1.2 dense UI. It and [Typography Master](https://www.typographymaster.com/guide/type-scale-systems) say the golden ratio is too large for UI.
- Size count. Video said a landing page uses at most 6 sizes. Upstream distill.md caps all UI at 3 or 4. We scope the count by mode. A full Persuade page starts at 6 at most, and a simple page uses about 3 ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition)). The 6-size cap and the 24px dashboard text cap are starting values.
- Line height. Video said 110 to 120% for large display headings. [Material](https://m3.material.io/styles/typography/applying-type) uses about 1.2 for large type and about 1.5 for body copy. Upstream typeset.md says tune per face, not a universal ratio. We start body at 1.45 to 1.6 and headings at 1.1 to 1.3, tighter as size grows. Layout must hold at a user line height of 1.5 ([WCAG text spacing](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/text-spacing.html)).
- Fluid type. Video used linear scaling from 320px to 1920px. Upstream operate.md keeps Operate on a fixed rem scale. We allow clamp() only on Persuade and Read, with rem bounds, a maximum of 2 to 2.5 times the minimum, and no vw-only sizes. Test at 200% zoom ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/modern-web-guidance/guides/accessibility/respect-os-text-scale), [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Values/clamp)). [Carbon](https://v10.carbondesignsystem.com/guidelines/typography/productive) keeps fluid type out of product UI.
- Tracking. Display text at 60 to 90px takes about -1%, per [Carbon's styles](https://github.com/carbon-design-system/carbon/blob/main/packages/type/scss/_styles.scss). Small text takes slight positive tracking. No all-caps value is set, because research found none.
- Weight. Size and weight carry hierarchy. Bold always carries more emphasis than a lighter weight at the same size ([Carbon](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/foundations/typography)). Thin fonts stay out of body text ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/GL/WCAG3/2020/methods/font-characteristics-contrast)). Opacity as hierarchy needs a contrast check on each background.
- Numbers. Right-align numbers, left-align text, and use tabular numerals ([GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/table), [UX Pickle](https://uxpickle.com/ux-best-practice-for-data-table-cell-content-alignment)).
- Eyebrow text. Video used a 12px eyebrow above headings. Upstream craft-floor.md bans eyebrows, and no brief earns them back. The ban stands.

## Interaction and navigation

- Competing clicks. Video said wait 300 ms or 700 ms to tell a single click from a double click. No source found for either. We wait the platform double-click time, 500 ms on Windows ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winuser/nf-winuser-setdoubleclicktime)), and only when one target has both a single-click and a double-click action. Non-competing actions run at once.
- Commit on release. Video said a popover trigger commits on mouse down, so a press, drag, and release can select. [W3C pointer cancellation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-cancellation.html) says activation belongs on the up-event. Buttons and menu items commit on release, and releasing outside cancels. A press alone runs nothing. Hold-to-switch shortcuts commit on key release and cancel on Escape: no source found.
- Safe triangle. Keep a submenu open while the pointer stays in the triangle to its edges, and switch at once outside it ([analysis of Amazon's mega-dropdown](https://bjk5.com/post/44698559168/breaking-down-amazons-mega-dropdown)). The speed cutoff and the edge-hover hold time have no source found.
- Tooltip delay. Video said 1000 ms. [GitLab Pajamas](https://design.gitlab.com/components/tooltip) and [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/controls/ttm-setdelaytime) both use 500 ms. We use about 500 ms.
- Neighbour tooltips. Video said adjacent controls open with no delay. [Radix](https://www.radix-ui.com/primitives/docs/components/tooltip) keeps a 300 ms window: the next trigger opens at once if the pointer arrives within 300 ms of leaving the last one. We use 300 ms. Keyboard focus shows a tooltip at once: no source found.
- Tooltip hiding. Leaving the trigger hides the tooltip unless the pointer moves onto it. Escape or focus loss hides it. [WCAG 1.4.13](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/content-on-hover-or-focus.html) requires tooltips to be dismissible, hoverable, and persistent.
- Undo instead of confirm. Video said routine deletes run at once with an undo toast, and no confirm dialog. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog) says the same: confirm only irreversible or costly actions. The undo window is 5 s by default. [Gmail](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/2819488) offers 5, 10, 20, or 30 s, and [Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gmail/how-to-unsend-email-gmail/) gives 5 s as the default. Pausing the timer while the toast is hovered or focused has no source found. Keeping the item in Trash after the toast closes has no source found either.
- Recently Deleted. Video used a 30-day retention window. No source found. It is a product policy, so confirm it with the owner before you set it.
- Optimistic action. Show a routine action as done at once while the real call runs. No source found.
- Autosave. Autosave where the system can save safely, and show the saved state ([Fluent 2](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/components/web/react/core/button/usage)). Use explicit Save for multi-select and multi-field commits ([Primer](https://primer.style/product/ui-patterns/saving)). Version history has no source found.
- Modal for a create form. Video said a centered modal fits a few-field create form. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modal-nonmodal-dialog) fits modals to important warnings, to information needed to continue a user-started process, and to splitting a complex workflow, and warns that they hide the context behind them. We keep create and edit forms inline or in a non-modal side panel, so the page stays visible, and allow a modal for a short required step.
- Toasts. Video said use toasts for warnings and errors. [Material 3](https://m3.material.io/components/snackbar/accessibility) says actionable snackbars should not auto-dismiss, and [Atomica](https://www.atomica11y.com/accessible-web/toast-snackbar/) says an error needing action belongs in an alert dialog. We keep those errors inline or in a dialog. A toast with no action auto-dismisses in 4 to 10 s ([Material 3](https://m3.material.io/components/snackbar/guidelines)). The undo toast is the one actionable toast, and it stays for its full window. Upstream android.md keeps snackbars for transient feedback.
- Navigation position. Video said keep navigation at the top. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/menu-design) puts primary nav in the header on websites and on the left in applications. Upstream adapt.md also endorses side nav on desktop. We use header nav on Persuade pages and left nav on Operate apps.
- Keyboard shortcuts. motion.md gives no animation to keyboard actions. Video had animation on shortcuts. We allow it only on a surface that teaches shortcuts, such as onboarding or docs.

## Product patterns

### Icons and labels

- Icon plus text. Video said replace "From" and "To" text with directional icons joined by a line. We keep the text. A text label must sit beside an icon ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability)), and upstream clarify.md says not to rely on iconography alone. Header actions use icon plus text, or icon plus count plus an accessible name.
- Icon-only navigation is out. Video said universal icons (Home, Bookmark, User) need no label, and a collapsed sidebar can show icons only. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability) says "universal" icons are rare, so every navigation icon gets a visible label. The owner had accepted the video's version, and research overruled it.
- Icon-only controls. Each has an accessible name and a tooltip. Video said so too.
- One icon family. Video allowed different icon styles in separate zones. Upstream craft-floor.md asks for one stroke and weight. [Material](https://m3.material.io/styles/icons/designing-icons) asks for consistent stroke weight and terminals. We keep one family.
- Check marks. Video used check glyphs as bullets. Upstream craft-floor.md bans glyphs that stand in for the icon set. Use an SVG check from the icon set.
- Keyboard shortcut labels in menus. Right-align them. No source found.

### Lists, tables, and cards

- Dividers. Video said remove dividers between list rows. Upstream surfaces.md keeps dividers. We use whitespace first. When rows pack tightly, add dividers as a starting choice, or zebra striping. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/data-tables) says striping can help. [CSS Wizardry](https://csswizardry.com/2010/08/zebra-striping-rows-and-columns) calls the research on striping inconclusive. Dividers over whitespace have no source found.
- Long lists. Use a Load More button, not infinite scroll. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/infinite-scrolling-tips) says infinite scroll can make the footer unreachable. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/alternatives-pagination-listing-pages) says infinite loading works for about 40 items or fewer per page.
- Clickable cards. Video said the whole card is clickable, with a hover arrow. We put one link on the card title and extend its hit area over the card. We do not wrap the card in a link or button ([Inclusive Components](https://inclusive-components.design/cards/), [NZ Government](https://govtnz.github.io/web-a11y-guidance/wct/cards)). Keyboard focus shows the same cue as hover, since upstream adapt.md says not to rely on hover for function.

### Search, empty states, and onboarding

- Empty states. Say why the space is empty, and give one primary action ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design), [Carbon](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/core/patterns/empty-states)).
- No results. Say there are no matches. Offer a spelling fix or broader terms ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/search-no-results-serp), [Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/no-results-page)). Keeping the query in the box and offering a clear control have no source found.
- Onboarding. No blocking tour. Show each hint at first use, and make every hint dismissable and recallable ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/onboarding-tutorials), [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/help-and-documentation)). The checklist pattern has no source found.
- Forms. Put a visible label above every input. A placeholder shows an example format and never replaces the label ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/form-design-placeholders), [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Attributes/placeholder)).
- Pricing. Use 3 to 4 tiers, and give one tier a contrast badge ([Figma](https://www.figma.com/resource-library/pricing-page-best-practices), [Mainsail](https://mainsailpartners.com/designing-an-effective-pricing-page-for-your-saas-business)). Hick's Law and explicit discount display have no source found.

### Loading and errors

- Wait times. Under 1 s, show no indicator. From 1 to 10 s, show a spinner on a button or one module. Past 10 s, show percent done with a clear cancel ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits), [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators)).
- Skeletons. NN/g limits skeleton screens to full-page loads ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens)). Upstream operate.md also uses them for content regions. We keep both. The upstream text stays, and this difference is recorded here.
- Missing page (404). Video said a playful 404 with conversational error copy. Upstream clarify.md says warmth is welcome and jokes are not. We allow a warm 404 that names the way back. No source found for the wording.

## Gestures and motion

### Gestures

- Visible control for every gesture. Video said pair every swipe with a tap target. No source found beyond the general target-size rules below.
- Carousel arrows. Video said remove carousel arrows where swipe works. We keep visible previous and next buttons, because keyboard and mouse users cannot swipe. [WCAG 2.1.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard.html) requires all functionality to work from a keyboard.
- Carousel motion. Prefer manual carousels ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/auto-forwarding)). Auto-rotation that runs past 5 s needs a visible pause control ([WCAG 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22)).
- Long-press and hover blur. Video used blur in hero, hover, and long-press effects. Upstream craft-floor.md bans decorative blur. We fade or dim instead. Long-press dims the background with a scrim, not blur. The hover blur ban is the same rule.

### Motion

- Bounce. Video used bouncy springs on tooltips and swipes, and an elastic snap at the top of an expansion. Upstream animate.md says not to use bounce or elastic curves by reflex. We use bounce 0 on UI chrome and tooltips, and a low bounce only on gesture release. [WWDC23](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10158) says springs need not be bouncy. [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion) says to avoid motion on frequent interactions and to let people cancel it. The 0.1-0.3 bounce range has no source found.
- Parallax and idle loops. Video had a bobbing balloon and scroll parallax on margin elements. Upstream animate.md calls purposeless decoration debt. Upstream overdrive.md allows scroll parallax as an option, so this override is narrow. We allow no scroll jacking and no parallax on Operate or Read. On a Persuade page, one authored moment may use parallax or an idle loop. It stops offscreen and under reduced motion. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101) says scroll jacking can do more harm than good. [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration) says parallax can make users dizzy.
- Particle burst. Video had confetti on success. Upstream overdrive.md says a particle system on a settings page looks embarrassing. We allow bursts only for rare, earned milestones, never routine actions. No source found for celebration guidance.
- Shimmer. Video had a moving shimmer across a border. Upstream craft-floor.md bans scattered effects. We allow a shimmer on a loading skeleton only, and stop it under reduced motion. No source found.
- Signature motion. Video said one or two signature motions repeated across the site. Upstream craft-floor.md allows one authored moment and bans identical entrances on every section. We let one signature motion recur at meaningful moments, such as a section change.
- Centerpiece. Video ran a second loop beside a 3D centerpiece. Upstream craft-floor.md allows one authored moment. The centerpiece is that moment, so no other loop runs beside it.

## Charts

- Bars start at zero. [Storytelling with Data](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2012/09/bar-charts-must-have-zero-baseline) and [ONS](https://service-manual.ons.gov.uk/data-visualisation/guidance/axes-and-gridlines) both say a bar axis never starts above zero. Line charts may use a non-zero axis.
- Axes and gridlines. Show a value axis, or direct labels when every mark has one. Gridlines are faint gray, few, and at round intervals. Drop them when every point is labeled ([ONS](https://service-manual.ons.gov.uk/data-visualisation/guidance/axes-and-gridlines), [Evergreen](https://stephanieevergreen.com/four-ways-to-show-projections/)).
- Line shape. Connect real points with straight segments. Smooth only a continuous measure, and never use a cardinal or natural spline ([Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/academy/what-to-consider-when-creating-line-charts)). Never join points across a gap in the data ([UK Government Analysis Function](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/data-visualisation-charts/)). Show 4 lines or fewer, or use small multiples.
- Actual and projected data. Do not fade actual data, especially the latest points. Dash or fade only projected segments, mark the boundary with a highlight band, and label the last value directly ([Evergreen](https://stephanieevergreen.com/four-ways-to-show-projections/), [data.europa](https://data.europa.eu/apps/data-visualisation-guide/direct-labelling)).
- Bar corners. Use square bar tops. Allow a few pixels of corner radius at most ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/data/charts/bar-chart-complete-guide), [Tabulareditor](https://tabulareditor.com/blog/building-better-bar-charts-in-power-bi-reports-a-comprehensive-guide)).
- Decoration. Every bar encodes one data value. No bars for padding or texture ([Wikipedia on chartjunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/en:Chartjunk), after Tufte). One study found embellishment aided recall ([Bateman et al.](https://vis.csail.mit.edu/classes/6.859/readings/pdfs/Bateman-UsefulJunk.pdf)). We keep the rule.
- Comparison series. Show the previous period in muted gray. Label each line at its end. Keep a legend only where direct labels collide ([Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/10-ways-to-use-fewer-colors-in-your-data-visualizations), [EU Data Visualisation Guide](https://data.europa.eu/apps/data-visualisation-guide/direct-labelling)).
- Categorical colors. Never use a brand ramp for unordered categories. Vary hue and lightness, and do not encode meaning in color alone ([Carbon](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/data-visualization/color-palettes)). Research put the cap near 7 categories ([Scanhue](https://scanhue.com/articles/data-visualization-color-palettes), a secondary source). Carbon allows up to 14. The dataviz skill owns the palette ladder, so check its number there.
- Pie charts. Aim for 4 slices, and never more than 5. [Datawrapper's academy](https://www.datawrapper.de/academy/what-to-consider-when-creating-a-pie-chart) says four, and [its blog](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/pie-charts) says five. Group the tail into "Other", or use bars when readers must compare shares ([Storytelling with Data](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2020/5/14/what-is-a-pie-chart)).
- Diverging values. Use one horizontal bar per category, centered on zero, with contrasting colors for the two signs. Do not stack diverging segments ([FT Visual Vocabulary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Financial-Times/chart-doctor/main/visual-vocabulary/Visual-vocabulary-en.pdf), and [Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/divergingbars), which argues against stacking).
- Micro-charts. Video said color the functional micro-charts, not rainbow buttons. Upstream craft-floor.md bans decorative stand-ins for content. A micro-chart that shows real data stays, with its value printed beside it. A loan card's progress diagram that encodes real repayment data also stays.
- Tooltips. Show the exact value, unit, and date on hover and on focus ([Carbon](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/data-visualization/chart-anatomy)). Label key points directly, because a tooltip shows one point at a time ([Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/10-ways-to-use-fewer-colors-in-your-data-visualizations)). Video said a line chart shows a percentage change bubble on hover, which we keep as a starting value. Dimming other bars on hover has no source found.
- KPI comparison. Show each KPI with a comparison: the prior period or a target, the change in absolute terms and in percent, and the direction. A raw number alone does not ship ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/choosing-chart-types/), [Tableau](https://www.tableau.com/kpi/what-is-kpi-dashboard)).
- Time ranges. Time-series charts get a range control with presets and a custom range. Buttons or a dropdown depend on the preset count, and research found no source that decides it. Test the choice with users.
- Familiar forms. Default to line, bar, and table. Use an unfamiliar form only when it answers the question better, and explain how to read it on the chart ([Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/chart-types-guide), [Tableau](https://help.tableau.com/current/blueprint/en-us/bp_visual_best_practices.htm)).
- Maps. Pair every choropleth with a sortable table of region, value, and unit ([visualizing.org](https://www.visualizing.org/choropleth-map)). Map rates, not totals. Use a bar chart when only a few regions have data ([ONS](https://service-manual.ons.gov.uk/data-visualisation/chart-types/choropleth-maps)).

## AI interface

- Entry. The prompt box is the entry point. Show 3 to 5 contextual suggestions, 6 at most: the page gives three-to-five in its summary and three to six in its design notes. It lets a suggestion either prefill the prompt or run at once; we prefill unless the action is cheap and easy to undo ([shapeof.ai suggestions](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/suggestions), [open input](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/open-input)).
- Modes. Show the active mode, and put a visible guardrail on costly modes ([shapeof.ai modes](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/modes)). @-mentions have no source found, so add them only where context needs them.
- Streaming. Stream output as it generates. Show a status label until the first token arrives ([Cloudscape](https://cloudscape.design/gen-ai/patterns/generative-ai-loading-states/)). Track time to first token as the latency target ([CHI 2026](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3772318.3790716)).
- Waiting text. Streamed AI text shows a status label, not a placeholder layout ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens), [Cloudscape](https://cloudscape.design/gen-ai/patterns/generative-ai-loading-states/)). Layout-matching placeholders fit only content with a known shape, such as tables or cards. Past 10 s, show progress with an estimate ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/progress-indicators)). Three bouncing dots beside the status label is a starting value, modeled on Notion AI. No source found.
- Sources and reasoning. Show the sources inline and as a list. Collapse reasoning by default with an expand control, and keep it live during long tasks. Flag a missing source, and never fill it in ([shapeof.ai stream of thought](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/stream-of-thought), [references](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/references)).
- Confidence. Show confidence only after a user test shows it helps. Pick the format by test: categorical (High, Medium, Low) or numeric (80%). State what the value means ([Google PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/chapters/explainability-trust)). The confidence pill wording and the click-to-reveal percentage are starting values.
- Inline refine. Revise only the selected part. Show the result as a preview with accept and reject, and never overwrite the original before accept ([shapeof.ai inline action](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/inline-action)).
- Memory. List every saved entry, and let the user view, edit, and delete each one. Show an inline notice when a memory is saved. Offer a memory-off mode ([shapeof.ai memory](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/memory)).
- Pastes and attachments. OpenAI turns a paste over 10,000 characters into an attachment, and "Show in text field" restores it ([release notes, as of Aug 2026](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes)). Our 10,000-character threshold is a starting value. Thumbnail previews have no source found.
- History. Make history searchable. Allow per-item delete and archive, and generate a title that the user can rename ([OpenAI](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8809935-deleting-and-archiving-chats-in-chatgpt)). A snippet under each title has no source found.
- Cost. Show a low-high estimate beside the send button, and update it when options change. List the drivers: prompt, context, and output. Estimates are based on max tokens or typical lengths ([shapeof.ai cost estimates](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/cost-estimates)). No dollar threshold is sourced.

## Landing

- Bold numbers. Video said consolidate copy into text blocks with bold numbers. Upstream craft-floor.md bans the hero-metric template, not bold numerals. We allow bold numbers inside text.
- Illustration. Video had doodles and scribbles around hero copy, and pencil scribbles under headlines. Upstream craft-floor.md bans sketch-style SVG scenes. We allow real illustration or none. Hand-drawn marks need a pinned brief that earns them (SKILL.md).
- Hero spacing and copy length. The 8px, 32px, and 32px gaps, a heading of about 7 words, a subtext of about 14 words, and a 1920 by 1000 desktop hero are starting values. No source found.

## Mobile and platforms

- Tab bar count. Video said 3 or 4 icons, with 5 as the absolute maximum. [Material](https://m3.material.io/components/navigation-bar/overview) sets 3 to 5 destinations of equal importance on Android. [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars) says five or fewer for customizable tabs, and our phone file uses that cap. Upstream ios.md says 2 to 5 and stays unchanged.
- Touch targets. Video said 44px. We use 44 pt on iOS ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)) and 48 dp on Android ([Material](https://m3.material.io/foundations/designing/structure)). Pointer targets are 44 dp. The web floor is 24 by 24 CSS px ([WCAG 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)).
- Base text. Body text is 17 pt on iOS and 13 pt on macOS ([Apple typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/foundations/typography)).
- macOS panels. Video said blur material in macOS panels. macOS provides standard system materials with designated purposes ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)), so a panel may use them. Custom glass needs a glass world (see color and surfaces).
- macOS window layout. Video said three zones: a top bar, a left sidebar, and a center canvas. No source found.
- Phone structure. One job per screen, a bottom sheet for a mid-task choice, and thumb reach all came from the video. No source found.
- Feeds and stories. Autoplay video needs a visible pause control ([WCAG 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22)). The other feed and story rules have no source found.
